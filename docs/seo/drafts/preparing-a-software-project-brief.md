# What to include in a software project brief

Editorial draft, not published. Proposed slug: preparing-a-software-project-brief. Studio voice; confirm CMS author. Derived from current service scope; no fixed-price or delivery-date promises.

A useful brief explains the users, the task and what completion means. You do not need to choose every technology before talking to a development partner. You do need enough context to distinguish the first release from later ideas.

## Describe the users and their main task

Who is the product for? What do they need to do? Describe the current process and where it becomes difficult. For a website, explain the offer and the visitor's next step. For an application, describe the core workflow from its first screen to its useful result.

For [Web Development](/services/web-development), bring existing content, required pages and integrations. For [Mobile App Development](/services/mobile-app-development), identify the platforms and important device workflows. Store submission support can be scoped, but store approval remains the platform's decision.

## Separate the first release from later work

List the journeys needed for the initial release and the ideas that can wait. Explain how the team will review a completed journey. Include loading, empty and error states where they affect the user's task.

If AI is involved, distinguish an [agent workflow](/services/ai-agent-development) from a [user-facing AI feature](/services/ai-product-engineering). Define approved information, allowed actions and the behavior expected when a result is wrong or unavailable.

## Identify existing systems and responsibilities

List the systems that need to connect, who owns them and whether documentation is available. Share public examples or sanitized materials first; do not send passwords, keys or production customer data in an initial brief.

For an existing platform, an [assessment or modernization phase](/services/platform-modernization) may be more useful than assuming a full rebuild. If the main uncertainty is a technical decision, an [Architecture & Governance review](/services/architecture-governance) can define options, trade-offs and next actions.

## Explain budget and timing constraints

State the budget range and any date that matters to the business. These are planning inputs, not an automatic price or delivery commitment. Scope, integrations, content readiness and release requirements need discussion before dates and costs are confirmed. Hosting, third-party services and ongoing support should be considered separately.

## Agree on handover

Ask which source code, build or setup instructions, test results and operating documents belong in the delivery. Clarify hosting ownership and what support is needed after handover. Completion and ongoing support should be explicit in the project scope.
